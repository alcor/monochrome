Harbor ferry returns after 14 years

By Mara Okafor · October 28, 2026

Share this article: FacebookXEmail

The cross-harbor ferry carried paying passengers on Monday for the first time since 2012, ending a 14-year gap that began when the old operator went out of business. The 38-meter vessel Marigold made its first crossing at 6:40 a.m. with 212 people on board, according to the harbor authority.

The service links the north pier to Saltmarsh Landing in 22 minutes. The same trip by road takes about 55 minutes at rush hour, because the only bridge is 9 kilometers upstream.

What it cost

The city council approved $18.6 million for the project in March 2024. Of that, $11.2 million paid for the vessel, which was built in a yard 300 kilometers down the coast, and the rest went to rebuilding both landings.

A one-way ticket costs $4.50, and a monthly pass costs $96. Children under 12 ride free, and bicycles are carried at no charge.

Who will ride it

The harbor authority expects 1,900 passengers on a weekday in the first year and has set a target of 2,600 by 2029. A survey of 840 households in Saltmarsh found that 31% would use the ferry at least once a week.

Twelve crossings run in each direction on weekdays and eight on weekends. The last boat leaves the north pier at 9:15 p.m.

What happens next

A second vessel is due in the spring of 2027, which would cut the wait between boats from 40 minutes to 20. The council will review the fares after six months of ticket data.
